A porcelain crown is a custom-made cap that covers a tooth to restore its strength, shape, and appearance. Crowns are often used when a tooth has a large fracture, extensive decay, or has become brittle — such as after a root canal — and needs full coverage to hold together and function normally.

## When a crown is the right call

Covering a tooth means removing more structure than a filling or onlay would, so we don't recommend a crown until it's genuinely warranted. In keeping with our conservative approach, we first consider whether a filling, inlay, or onlay could protect the tooth while preserving more of it. When a tooth truly needs full coverage to stay healthy and usable, a crown is the restoration that protects it best.

## Who it's a good fit for

Crowns are a good fit when a significant portion of the tooth is compromised but the root and surrounding bone are healthy enough to support a restoration. During your exam we'll show you what we see and explain why a crown is — or isn't — the most appropriate step.

## What to expect

Placing a crown typically takes a couple of visits. We'll gently prepare the tooth, take an impression or digital scan, and often place a temporary crown while your permanent one is made. At the follow-up visit we fit the finished porcelain crown, check your bite, and adjust it so it feels natural and comfortable.
