Inlays and onlays are custom-made restorations that repair a tooth damaged by decay or a crack when a simple filling isn't enough — but a full crown would remove more healthy tooth than necessary. An inlay fits within the cusps on the biting surface, while an onlay extends over one or more of those cusps.

## A tooth-preserving middle ground

We lean on inlays and onlays because they fit our conservative approach: they restore strength and function while keeping as much of your natural tooth as possible. Made from tooth-colored porcelain or composite, they're bonded securely in place and blend in so the repair isn't obvious.

## Who it's a good fit for

They're often the right choice when a large old filling has failed, or when decay or a fracture affects a meaningful part of a back tooth that still has healthy structure worth saving. During your exam we'll look closely at how much sound tooth remains and recommend the least-invasive option that will actually last.

## What to expect

Placing an inlay or onlay usually takes a couple of visits. We'll gently prepare the tooth, take an impression or digital scan, and fit the finished restoration at a follow-up appointment, checking your bite so everything feels comfortable and natural. If we ever find that a filling would serve you just as well, we'll tell you.
