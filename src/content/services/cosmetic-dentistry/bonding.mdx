Bonding uses tooth-colored resin to repair and reshape a tooth. We place it directly on the tooth, shape it by hand, and harden it with a curing light. It suits small problems: a chip, a crack along an edge, a little gap, a rough spot, or one discolored area.

## Why we often start here

Usually little or no enamel has to come off, and bonding can often be reversed later. For many small cosmetic fixes it's the least invasive way to get a visible improvement. So before we talk about veneers or crowns, we tend to ask whether bonding would do.

Say you chipped the corner of a front tooth on a water bottle. In a case like that, we can often rebuild the corner with resin in one visit, matched to the shade around it. The rest of the tooth stays as it was.

## Where resin has limits

Resin is softer than porcelain and picks up stains more easily. Coffee, tea, wine, and tobacco can darken it over time, and it can chip, especially on the edges you bite with. The good news is that touch-ups and repairs are simple. For bigger changes across several teeth, or spots that take heavy chewing force, we'll tell you honestly whether bonding will hold up or whether something else would serve you better.

Most bonding takes a single visit, often without numbing. We match the resin to your shade, shape and harden it, then polish it so it blends with the enamel around it. Before you leave, we'll go over how to keep it looking fresh.
