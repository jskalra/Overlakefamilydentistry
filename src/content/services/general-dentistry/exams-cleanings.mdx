Most visits are uneventful, and that's the point. A regular exam and cleaning gives us a look at your teeth and gums a couple of times a year, so small problems get found while they're still small.

## What happens in the chair

One of our hygienists starts by removing plaque and tartar from your teeth and along the gumline. Tartar is plaque that has hardened, and once it forms, a toothbrush won't get it off. After that comes polishing, then a few minutes on your brushing and flossing if there's something worth adjusting.

Then the exam. We check each tooth, your gums, and how your bite fits together, and we do an oral cancer screening. X-rays happen only when they'll tell us something we can't see by looking. Plenty of visits don't need them.

## Why come in when nothing hurts

Early cavities and gum disease usually don't hurt. By the time a tooth aches, the problem has often grown past the point where a small filling would fix it.

Say it's been two years since your last cleaning, and your gums bleed a bit when you floss. At the visit we might find some tartar below the gumline and one spot of early decay. A thorough cleaning and a small filling would likely take care of both. Wait another couple of years and that same tooth could need much more work.

That's why we see children, adults, and longtime patients on the same routine. If we do spot something, you'll hear about it in plain terms, with the simplest option first. Nobody will rush you into a decision.
