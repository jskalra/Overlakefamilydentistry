Scaling and root planing is a cleaning that goes below the gumline. People often call it a "deep cleaning." It treats gum disease in its earlier stages, before it damages the bone that holds your teeth in place.

## How it differs from a regular cleaning

A routine cleaning works on the teeth and just at the gumline. With gum disease, plaque and tartar build up under the gums, and the gums start pulling away from the teeth. That creates pockets where bacteria collect, and a regular cleaning can't reach the bottom of them. Bleeding when you brush, tender gums, and bad breath that doesn't go away are common signs.

We measure those pockets and review X-rays before recommending anything. If a regular cleaning is enough, we'll say so.

Say your gums have bled when you brush for months and your last visit showed a few deeper pockets around the back teeth. That's the kind of situation where a deep cleaning makes sense. Left alone, those pockets tend to get deeper.

The visit itself starts with numbing the area. Scaling removes the hardened deposits from the tooth and below the gumline. Root planing smooths the root surfaces, which helps the gums heal and reattach. Some people finish in one visit. Others come back for a second, with one side of the mouth done at a time.

## The first few days, and the long run

Your gums may feel tender for a few days, and teeth can be sensitive to hot or cold. Over-the-counter pain relief usually handles it. Over the next few weeks, healthy gums tend to feel firmer and bleed less.

Gum disease is a long-term condition, so the results depend on what happens next. Most patients move to a maintenance cleaning schedule, and daily brushing and flossing at home matter a lot. Keeping up with both is the best way to avoid more involved treatment later.
