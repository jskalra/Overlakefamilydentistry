An extraction is the removal of a tooth. We view it as a last resort and recommend it only when a tooth can't be saved, for example due to severe decay, advanced gum disease, a fracture, or crowding that affects your bite.

## When an extraction is needed

Sometimes a tooth is too damaged to repair, or removing it protects the health of the teeth and gums around it. We'll always explain why an extraction is being recommended and review any tooth-preserving alternatives first, so the decision is one you feel good about.

## What to expect

The area is thoroughly numbed before we begin, so you'll feel pressure but not pain. Afterward, we'll give you simple aftercare instructions, mild soreness and some swelling for a few days is normal, and the socket heals as a clot forms and gum tissue closes over it.

We'll also talk through replacing the tooth when appropriate, since filling the space with an implant, bridge, or partial helps keep your bite stable and your neighboring teeth in place.
