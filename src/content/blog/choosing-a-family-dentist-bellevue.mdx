Moving to the Eastside? Here's what to look for in a dentist your whole family can trust for years.

## Start with how they recommend treatment

The most important thing to learn about a dentist is how they decide what you need. Ask whether they start with the least-invasive option, and whether they'll tell you when something can be watched rather than treated right away. A dentist who explains the reasoning behind each recommendation — and is comfortable with you asking questions or seeking a second opinion — is one you can trust over the long run.

## Look for a practice that sees the whole family

A family dentist cares for every age under one roof: a child's first visit, teenagers, adults, and seniors. That continuity is convenient, and it means one team gets to know your family's history over time. If you have young children, ask how the practice handles first visits and whether they take an unhurried, reassuring approach.

## Check what care is offered in-house

Practices vary in how much they handle themselves versus refer out. Cleanings, fillings, crowns, root canals, extractions, and cosmetic work under one roof means fewer referrals and a team that already knows your history. Ask what's done in-house and when they'd refer you to a specialist.

## Consider the practical details

For a dentist you'll visit for years, logistics matter:

- **Location and parking** — an easy drive and free parking make regular visits far more likely to happen.
- **Hours** — do they fit your schedule for exams and the occasional urgent visit?
- **Longevity of the team** — long-tenured staff usually signals a stable, well-run practice, and you'll see familiar faces each visit.
- **Reviews** — read what long-term patients say, not just recent ones.

## Trust the first visit

A good first appointment tells you a lot: whether you felt listened to, whether the exam was thorough, and whether the recommendations were explained clearly and without pressure. That's the experience you'll repeat for years, so weigh how it felt.
