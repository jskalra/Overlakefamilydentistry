Conservative dentistry means preserving as much of your natural tooth as possible. Here's what that looks like in practice.

## Treat what needs treating

Every tooth is made of tissue that doesn't grow back. Once enamel is drilled away or a tooth is removed, it's gone for good. Conservative dentistry starts from that fact: the goal is to keep your natural teeth healthy and intact for as long as possible, and to intervene only when there's a clear reason to.

In practice, that means we don't recommend a filling, crown, or replacement unless the problem in front of us calls for it. If a small spot can be watched rather than drilled, we watch it. If a tooth can be saved rather than pulled, we work to save it.

## The least-invasive option first

When treatment is needed, there's usually a range of ways to handle it. A conservative approach reaches for the option that removes the least healthy tooth structure and still solves the problem.

- A small area of decay may need only a modest filling rather than a crown.
- A cracked or worn tooth may be repairable with bonding before it needs anything larger.
- An infected tooth can often be saved with a root canal instead of being extracted.

The right choice depends on your specific situation, and we'll walk you through the trade-offs so the decision is yours.

## Why it matters over the long run

Your natural teeth support your bite, your jawbone, and the teeth around them. Preserving them tends to keep your mouth healthier and more comfortable over the years than replacing them. Working conservatively also means fewer large procedures, which most people prefer.

You should expect a clear explanation of what we see, an honest answer about whether treatment is needed now, and the most conservative option that fits. If you'd like a second opinion before committing to a larger procedure, we're glad to give one.
