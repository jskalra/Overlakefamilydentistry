Regular exams and cleanings catch small problems before they become big ones. Here's why prevention pays off.

## Small problems are cheaper and easier to fix

Most dental problems start small and quiet. A little decay, a bit of gum inflammation, or a hairline crack often causes no pain at first. Caught early, these are simple to treat — a modest filling, a deep cleaning, a small repair. Left alone, the same problems tend to grow into ones that need a crown, a root canal, or an extraction. Prevention keeps you on the less invasive, less costly end of that range.

## What a preventive visit actually does

A routine exam and cleaning is doing more than polishing your teeth:

- **The exam** looks for early decay, gum changes, worn or cracked teeth, and other issues while they're still small — often before you'd notice anything.
- **X-rays**, taken as needed, show problems between teeth and below the gumline that aren't visible otherwise.
- **The cleaning** removes the hardened plaque (tartar) that brushing can't, which is what drives gum disease over time.

Together, these catch issues at the stage where they're easiest to handle.

## Prevention protects more than teeth

Healthy gums are the foundation your teeth rest on, and gum disease is a leading cause of tooth loss in adults. Regular cleanings and early treatment keep that foundation sound. Keeping your natural teeth, in turn, supports your bite and jawbone for the long term.

## What you can do between visits

Most of prevention happens at home: brushing twice a day, cleaning between your teeth daily, and limiting frequent sugar. Regular visits catch what daily care can't reach, and let us coach you on the specific habits that will help most.

## How often is right for you

For many people, an exam and cleaning twice a year works well. Some need to come more often, and we'll base that on what we actually see rather than a fixed rule.
